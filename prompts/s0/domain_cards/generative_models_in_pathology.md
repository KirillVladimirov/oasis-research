Domain: **Generative Models in Pathology (generative_models_in_pathology)**. This domain studies content generation for pathology workflows, centered on high-resolution **whole-slide images (WSIs)** and the clinical constraints of stain/scanner variability, limited annotations, and cross-site generalization. Core elements include (i) **image generation and manipulation**: patch-to-WSI synthesis, mask-guided generation (image–mask pairs, inpainting), artifact restoration, high/multi-resolution synthesis, text-to-image generation, and **stain synthesis** (normalization, transfer, virtual staining); (ii) **text generation**: captioning, VQA, and clinical report generation/abstraction; (iii) **morphology ↔ molecular profile** cross-modality generation (virtual molecular profiling and reverse generation); and (iv) other generative tasks such as spatial layout generation and cell/tissue simulation. Methodologically, the space spans **GANs, VAEs, diffusion/score-based models, and foundation models (LLMs/VLMs)** adapted to pathology-specific fidelity requirements (cell-level structure, stain consistency, gigapixel context). A typical loop trains a (conditional) generative model on curated WSI patches (often with explicit conditioning on tissue type, grade, masks, or text prompts), generates synthetic samples for augmentation/normalization/virtual staining, applies quality control (automated or expert-in-the-loop) to filter artifacts, and then plugs the generated content into downstream discriminative tasks (classification/segmentation/prognosis) or domain adaptation; evaluation combines **generative fidelity/diversity** with **task-level utility** (improvement under data scarcity, imbalance, or domain shift), and increasingly considers robustness, fairness, and privacy-preserving sharing as first-class criteria.
