Domain: **Graph Neural Network Research (graph_nn_research)**. GNNs study representation learning and reasoning on graph-structured data via **message passing** over nodes and edges, enabling tasks such as node classification, link prediction, graph classification, and recommendation on homogeneous, heterogeneous, and temporal graphs. Core elements include message-passing and aggregation operators, attention mechanisms, positional/structural encodings, choices of receptive field depth (and related phenomena such as oversmoothing/oversquashing), supervision regimes (supervised, self-supervised/contrastive, pretraining), and robustness issues (noise, heterophily, domain shift). Systems-facing threads emphasize **scalability** (sampling/mini-batching, linearized models, partitioning), efficiency/quantization/compression, and distributed or **federated graph learning** under privacy and communication constraints. A typical loop defines graph construction and features, selects a GNN family and training objective, trains with neighbor sampling or full-batch propagation, validates under realistic graph splits, and iterates on architecture/regularization and scalability tactics; evaluation uses task metrics (e.g., accuracy/AUC), generalization across graphs/splits, and system metrics such as throughput, memory footprint, scaling efficiency, and cost.
