Domain: **Diffusion Models** (diffusion_models). Diffusion models are generative frameworks that learn to transform simple noise into data by **iterative denoising**, typically via a forward "noising" process and a learned reverse process parameterized by a neural network (often trained with **denoising/score-matching–style objectives**). Core elements include noise schedules and parameterizations (predicting noise/score/velocity), sampling algorithms (ancestral sampling, deterministic variants such as DDIM-like updates, and **SDE/ODE** formulations with predictor–corrector solvers), conditioning and control mechanisms (classifier/classifier-free guidance; conditioning on text, labels, layouts, masks), efficiency methods (distillation, solver-based acceleration, fewer function evaluations), and architectural choices enabling scalability (e.g., **latent diffusion** via an autoencoder bottleneck and cross-attention conditioning). A typical loop trains the denoiser on corrupted examples across timesteps, then generates by starting from Gaussian noise and repeatedly denoising (optionally with guidance/conditioning) until a clean sample emerges; evaluation emphasizes sample quality and diversity (e.g., FID/IS and task-specific perceptual metrics), controllability/fidelity to conditions, and **quality–compute trade-offs** (sampling steps, throughput, cost).
