Domain: **Causal Inference** (causal_inference). Causal inference develops formal frameworks and estimators for answering interventional and counterfactual questions (e.g., the effect of a treatment or policy) from experimental or observational data under explicit assumptions. Core elements include **causal graphs/DAGs** and **structural causal models** for encoding assumptions; the **potential outcomes** view for defining estimands such as ATE/CATE; **identification** (backdoor/frontdoor, instrumental variables, mediation) and assumptions (exchangeability/unconfoundedness, positivity, SUTVA, measurement error); and estimation strategies spanning matching/weighting (IPW), doubly robust methods, and modern causal ML (uplift, heterogeneous effects, representation learning), with related subtopics such as causal discovery and domain shift. A typical loop specifies a causal model and target estimand, checks identifiability, estimates the effect with one or more estimators, and then performs **refutation/sensitivity analyses** (placebo/negative controls, robustness to unobserved confounding) before reporting uncertainty and decision-relevant quantities; evaluation emphasizes bias/variance (or RMSE), confidence interval coverage, robustness, and out-of-distribution validity.
